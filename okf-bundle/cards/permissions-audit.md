## Why

Every app on your phone is a potential surveillance endpoint. Apps collect location, contacts, microphone, and camera data — often through the advertising ecosystem without the app developer even knowing. Data brokers buy this data and sell it to advertisers, law enforcement, and intelligence agencies. Fewer apps means fewer attack surfaces.

## Steps

### General

Go through every app on your phone. Uninstall anything you haven't used in the last 30 days. For apps you keep, review and revoke any permissions that aren't essential for the app's core function (location, camera, microphone, contacts, storage).

### Android

1. Uninstall apps you don't use: Settings → Apps → [app] → Uninstall. Long-press app icons on the home screen and drag to Uninstall for faster access.
2. Revoke permissions on apps you keep: Settings → Security & privacy → Privacy → Permission manager → Review each permission (location, camera, microphone, contacts, storage, phone). For each app, revoke anything not essential. Alternatively: Settings → Apps → [app] → Permissions → revoke individually.

### iOS

1. Uninstall apps you don't use: Long-press app icon → Remove App → Delete App.
2. Revoke permissions on apps you keep: Settings → Privacy & Security → Review each permission type (Location, Camera, Microphone, Contacts, Photos, etc.). For each, see which apps have access and revoke. Alternatively: Settings → [app] → toggle off individual permissions.
