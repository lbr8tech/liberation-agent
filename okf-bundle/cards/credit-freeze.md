## Why

If your personal information is exposed in a data breach — and it will be — an identity thief can use your name, Social Security number, and address to open new credit accounts, take out loans, or sign up for utilities in your name. You may not find out until collections calls start or your credit score tanks. A credit freeze blocks creditors from accessing your credit report, which prevents new accounts from being opened at all. It's free and doesn't affect your existing accounts or credit score.

## Steps

### General

A credit freeze is a federal right under the FCRA. It blocks new creditors from accessing your credit report entirely. Don't confuse it with a credit lock — locks are commercial products governed by bureau terms of service, not federal law, and the bureau can change the terms at any time. Use a freeze, not a lock.

Contact all three bureaus separately — freezing one does not freeze the others:
1. Equifax: equifax.com/personal/credit-report-services/credit-freeze/ or call 888-298-0045
2. Experian: experian.com/help/credit-freeze/ or call 888-397-3742
3. TransUnion: transunion.com/credit-freeze or call 800-916-8800

Free by federal law since September 2018. You can freeze and unfreeze online or by phone. Online is fastest — the bureau must place the freeze within one business day and lift it within one hour. When you need to apply for credit, temporarily thaw only the bureau the lender will check, then re-freeze.
